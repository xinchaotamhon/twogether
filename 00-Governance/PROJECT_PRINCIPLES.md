# Project Principles

- Optimize for durable understanding and healthy study habits, not raw activity, XP, streak length, or card volume.
- Preserve learner agency: each person can choose a realistic daily goal, pause without shame, inspect why a card is due, and delete or export their private review history.
- Use active retrieval before feedback. The app may help with a hint, but it must not make recognition look like recall.
- Keep a principle-to-mechanism-to-boundary-to-transfer ladder. A principle without an example and a counterexample is not accepted as mastered.
- Separate shared content from private learner state at the schema and authorization layers.
- Prefer a small reversible MVP. Offline review and notifications are separate increments because sync and permission failures can otherwise hide data loss.
- Treat FSRS as a scheduler, not a truth oracle. Preserve review logs, expose uncertainty, and allow correction of an accidental grade without rewriting history.
- Use gamification as a gentle invitation: personal consistency, cooperative sibling goals, and mastery progress first; zero-sum ranking is opt-in and never the primary score.
- Accessibility is part of correctness: semantic controls, keyboard operation, focus visibility, reduced motion, color-independent status, readable Vietnamese/English copy, and screen-reader labels are gates.
- External source claims, AI suggestions, and user preferences remain labeled. Missing Claude content stays unknown rather than being filled with plausible prose.
